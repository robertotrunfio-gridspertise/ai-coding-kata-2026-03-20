Strategy can work here, but only if you apply it narrowly.

Used well, Strategy is a good fit for customer-type behavior. Used everywhere, it becomes heavier than this kata needs.

**Where Strategy Fits**
The strongest fit is customer behavior.

Customer type currently controls several related decisions:
- base discount
- Black Friday discount behavior
- free shipping threshold
- shipping surcharge exception
- tax exception

Those are coherent behaviors owned by the customer type, so Strategy maps well.

For example, conceptually:
- `VipPolicy`
- `PremiumPolicy`
- `EmployeePolicy`
- `RegularPolicy`
- `NewPolicy`
- `UnknownCustomerPolicy`

Then the calculator asks the selected strategy:
- what is the base discount
- what is the Black Friday adjustment
- does this customer get free shipping at this discounted subtotal
- does this customer add a shipping surcharge
- does this customer override tax

That is a legitimate Strategy use.

**Why Strategy Alone Is Not Enough**
The module is not driven only by customer type.

Behavior also depends on:
- coupon code
- country
- Black Friday
- ordering between shipping and tax rules

So if you model everything as Strategy, you usually end up with one of these problems:

1. too many strategies
   - customer strategy
   - coupon strategy
   - country strategy
   - shipping strategy
   - tax strategy

2. hidden orchestration
   - the final behavior becomes harder to read than the current imperative code

3. poor fit for cross-cutting rules
   - `FREESHIP` affects shipping
   - `TAXFREE` affects tax
   - `SAVE10` affects discount
   These are better modeled as focused rules than as one giant strategy hierarchy.

So Strategy is good for one axis of variation, not the whole design.

**Best Use Of Strategy Here**
I would use:

1. Strategy for customer behavior
2. explicit pipeline/orchestrator for the overall flow
3. small rule objects or focused functions for coupons/promotions

That gives you this split:

- Customer Strategy:
  customer-specific behavior

- Promotion Rules:
  coupon/event-specific behavior

- Calculator Orchestrator:
  preserves execution order and combines everything

This is cleaner than a pure Strategy design.

**Why Customer Type Is The Right Strategy Boundary**
A strategy boundary is good when:
- behavior varies by one clear dimension
- that behavior is cohesive
- new variants should be addable independently

Customer type satisfies that.

Coupon code does not satisfy it as cleanly, because coupon effects are fragmented across:
- discount
- shipping
- tax

Country is even less attractive as a strategy boundary because it mainly supplies:
- base shipping
- base tax

That is closer to classification or lookup than a full behavior strategy.

**If You Want A Pure Strategy Version**
You could do this:

- `CustomerPricingStrategy` trait
- implementations per customer type
- factory from normalized customer type string

Trait methods could be something like:
- `base_discount_percent`
- `black_friday_discount_percent`
- `apply_shipping_rules`
- `apply_tax_rules`

This would work.

But I would be careful:
- if `apply_shipping_rules` and `apply_tax_rules` get too big, you have simply moved the old complexity into strategy classes
- if coupon logic still sits elsewhere, then Strategy only solves part of the design

So pure Strategy is not wrong, just incomplete.

**Tradeoffs Of Strategy Here**
Pros:
- very natural for customer types
- makes adding a new customer type easier
- reduces `if customer_type == ...`
- improves local reasoning

Cons:
- does not solve coupon design by itself
- can introduce boilerplate quickly
- can hide business flow if overused
- may be more abstract than needed for a small kata

**My Recommendation**
Use Strategy, but only for customer behavior.

I would not describe the overall solution as “the Strategy pattern” only.
I would describe it as:

Strategy for customer policies, plus an explicit pricing pipeline.

That is the technically accurate answer.

If you want the simplest strong design, I would choose one of these two:

1. enum-based strategy
   - `CustomerKind` enum
   - methods on the enum
   - simpler, less boilerplate, very Rust-friendly

2. trait-based strategy
   - `CustomerPolicy` trait
   - concrete policy structs
   - a bit more textbook Strategy, but heavier

For this kata, I would prefer enum-based strategy first.
It preserves clarity and is easier to explain and maintain.

So the short answer is:
- yes, Strategy fits
- no, Strategy alone is not enough
- best result: Strategy for customer behavior, rules/functions for promotions, explicit orchestration for the pipeline
